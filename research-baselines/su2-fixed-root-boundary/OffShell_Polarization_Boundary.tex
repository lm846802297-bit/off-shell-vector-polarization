\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}

We study a null shift of two equal-mass Higgs momenta that leaves both
virtual vector momenta fixed. For a neutral vector bridge sewn to two
arbitrary conserved sources in a single-doublet, renormalizable broken
(SU(2)) theory, the complete reduced tree amplitude is bounded but
generically nonvanishing at infinity. We give its boundary as an
explicit bilinear in the currents and show that no generic shift
direction in this class removes it for every allowed pair of currents.
This obstruction persists even when the unphysical (R\_\xi) poles cancel
exactly. We then obtain the large-shift constant for a complete
Yukawa-coupled six-point tree, separating the bridge, single emission,
double emission from the same fermion line, and stationary scalar
splitting. Six moving poles plus the independently derived boundary
reconstruct the benchmark amplitude. The result identifies the extra
data needed under the fixed-root deformation, not a failure of all-line
transverse recursion or a definition of a physical spin density away
from a pole.

\subsection{Setup and prior work}\label{setup-and-prior-work}

Metric (g=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)), all momenta incoming, Fourier
waves (e\^{}\{-ipx\}). The scalar doublet has a radial Higgs (h) of mass
(M), vacuum expectation value (v), and a neutral (W\^{}3) of mass
(m=av), with (a=g\_2/2). Write (q,K) for the two end momenta and (p,r)
for the two Higgs momenta, (q+K+p+r=0),
(p\textsuperscript{2=r}2=M\^{}2). Internal exchanges have propagators
(1/(Q\textsuperscript{2-m}2)) and (1/(Q\textsuperscript{2-\xi m}2)); no
widths are used. A dot product is bilinear, with no complex conjugation.
The Higgs deformation is {[} p(z)=p+zn,\qquad r(z)=r-zn,\qquad
n\^{}2=n\cdot p=n\cdot r=0,\qquad d=2q\cdot n\ne0. {]} The test keeps
(q,K,p+r) fixed and excludes coincident moving poles and stationary
singularities. In the Higgs-pair rest frame the two projective null
choices are the complex linear combinations of the two spatial
directions transverse to the Higgs axis. Our result applies to both
choices when the nondegeneracy conditions hold.

Ke, Liu and Mahbub derive an off-shell continuation of the on-shell
transverse/longitudinal projectors and discuss the large-(z) conditions
for the \textbf{all-line transverse (ALT)} shift
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13096v1\#S3}{arXiv:2611.13096v1,
§§3--4 and App. B}. Their constructibility statements for ALT do not
imply that this two-Higgs shift has a vanishing boundary. Our earlier
\href{https://github.com/lm846802297-bit/off-shell-vector-polarization/tree/6d92e34be9694920dc0c64fd0a1d18747ccb6622/research-baselines/su2-fivepoint-fixedq-boundary}{fixed-root
check} tested the off-shell \emph{contracted} weak current against the
Goldstone current. Here we treat the physical contraction between two
sources and then the full signed six-point amplitude. We take the
on-shell pole factorization as given; the added result is the physical
boundary in this deformation class.

\subsection{Two-source boundary
theorem}\label{two-source-boundary-theorem}

Assume specified sources (j,e) with (q\cdot j=K\cdot e=0), which are
fixed under the shift and couple to the neutral bridge without
separately emitting (h). This includes two distinct zero-Yukawa fermion
currents in the stated neutral channel. The fixed root propagators and
overall phase are removed from the reduced amplitude (\mathcal B). Let
(Q\_\pm=q+p,q+r) at (z=0), (D\_\pm=Q\_\pm\textsuperscript{2-m}2), and
(s\_\{hh\}=(q+K)\^{}2). The sums of the two derivative Goldstone
vertices and the gauge-dependent part of the (R\_\xi) vector propagator
reduce the ordered numerator to the unitary expression, since {[}
\frac{1+m^2(1-\xi)/(Q^2-m^2)}{Q^2-\xi m^2} =\frac{1}{Q^2-m^2}. {]}
Adding the (WWhh) vertex and the stationary (h\^{}*\to hh) split then
gives {[} \mathcal B(z)= C\_0, j\cdot e+4a\^{}2\sum\emph{\{\sigma=\pm1\}
\frac{m^2 j\cdot e-[j\cdot(Q_\sigma+\sigma zn)]
[e\cdot(Q_\sigma+\sigma zn)]}{D_\sigma+\sigma dz},
\quad C\_0=2a\^{}2\left(1+\frac{3M^2}{s_{hh}-M^2}\right). {]} The
products quadratic in (z) are the same in the two orderings. Dividing by
their denominators, the terms linear in (z) cancel; the remaining
constant is {[} B}\infty=C\_0,j\cdot e -4a\^{}2\left[
  \frac{[j\cdot(q-K)](e\cdot n)+(j\cdot n)[e\cdot(q-K)]}{d}
  -\frac{(j\cdot n)(e\cdot n)(D_++D_-)}{d^2}
\right]. {]} Here (D\_++D\_-=2(M\textsuperscript{2-m}2-q\cdot K)). The
expression is unchanged by rescaling (n), but can change between the two
projective directions. Given the simple finite residues
(\rho\emph{\sigma) in the (z) plane, the exact recursive identity for
this reduced amplitude is {[}
\mathcal B(0)=B}\infty-\sum\emph{\sigma\frac{\rho_\sigma}{z_\sigma}. {]}
The scalar-splitting term in (B}\infty) has an (s\_\{hh\}=M\^{}2) pole
in the full amplitude. Calling all of (B\_\infty) a local contact
interaction would be incorrect: it is regular in (z), not necessarily in
the unshifted invariants.

\textbf{Obstruction valid for a class of sources.} At any admissible
(n), the Gram determinant of (q,K,n) is (- (q\cdot n)\^{}2 s\_\{hh\}).
If it is nonzero, the one-dimensional common orthogonal complement is
nondegenerate. Choose (j=e=t) in that complement, (t\^{}2\ne0). All
ordered boundary terms vanish and (B\_\infty=C\_0 t\^{}2\ne0) for
generic (s\_\{hh\}). Thus no admissible direction at generic kinematics
eliminates the boundary as a bilinear on the whole conserved-current
space. This is not a claim that every individual helicity choice has a
nonzero boundary. For timelike roots, varying the directions of a
massless fermion pair spans the transverse current space; the witness
can be understood as a linear combination of currents rather than a
single real helicity state.

\subsection{Complete six-point benchmark with nonzero Yukawa
coupling}\label{complete-six-point-benchmark-with-nonzero-yukawa-coupling}

We reuse the
\href{https://github.com/lm846802297-bit/off-shell-vector-polarization/tree/ba7dfb747da24809d1c1d9250bd9f8f6ce3677bf/research-baselines/su2-sixpoint-connected-tree}{locked
27-graph amplitude} for distinguishable fermion pairs. The (f) line has
(f\_L) in a doublet and (f\_R) a singlet with Yukawa mass (\mu); the far
line is massless and vectorlike. The near chiral current (j) need not be
conserved, while (K\cdot e=0). Strip no factors in this section. In
unitary gauge define {[} L=-a,\frac{j-q(q\cdot j)/m^2}{q^2-m^2},\qquad
R=-a,\frac{e}{K^2-m^2},\qquad W=\not R P\_L,\quad b=\frac{\mu a}{m}. {]}
In the signed amplitude convention of the benchmark, the bridge boundary
is (iB\_\infty[L,R]): the fixed root factors are now included in (L,R),
and the same polynomial division needs no additional conservation
condition in unitary gauge.

For the remaining classes, the hard fermion propagator obeys {[}
S(P+wn)=\frac{\not n}{\ell}+\frac{1}{w} \left(\frac{\not P+\mu}{\ell}
-\frac{(P^2-\mu^2)\not n}{\ell^2}\right)
+O(w\^{}\{-2\}),\qquad \ell=2P\cdot n. {]} Here (w) is (+z) or (-z), and
all hard-line slopes are taken nonzero. For a Higgs assignment (x=p) or
(r), set (k=q+x) and define (J\_0,J\_1) as the first two Laurent
coefficients of {[} J\^{}\alpha(w)=\bar v\_f{[} \gamma\^{}\alpha P\_L
S(f+x+wn) +S(f-k-wn)\gamma\^{}\alpha P\_L{]}u\_f . {]} The coefficient
(n\cdot J\_0) vanishes by (\not nP\_L\not n=0); the leading longitudinal
numerator in the internal unitary propagator therefore does not produce
a growing term in this class. Writing (g\_h=2iam), its constant is {[}
B\_\{\rm mixed\}=\sum\emph{\{x=p,r\}
\frac{g_h a b\,(R\cdot n)}{2m^2 k\cdot n}, (k\cdot J\_0+n\cdot J\_1).
{]} For the double line emission, write (U=\not n/(2f\cdot n)) and
(V=\not n/{[}2(f+K)\cdot n{]}). The two label orderings give the same
leading constant, since (\not n/\ell) is unchanged under (n\to-n): {[}
B}\{\rm double\}=-2iab\^{}2\bar v\_f\big[
 W S(f+p+r)U+V W U+V S(f+K)W\big]u\_f . {]} The line-emitted scalar
split is stationary and its value is its boundary: {[}
B\_\{\rm split\}=-iab\frac{3M^2}{v(s_{hh}-M^2)}
\bar v\_f\big[W S(f+p+r)+S(f+K)W\big]u\_f . {]} These four classes
exhaust the locked benchmark. In all classes the large-(z) amplitude is
bounded; the formula makes no assertion of separate gauge invariance of
the classes at finite (z).

\subsection{Numerical evidence and what remains
open}\label{numerical-evidence-and-what-remains-open}

The two-source formula was checked by separate finite (R\_\xi)
contractions, small contours at the physical and would-be gauge poles,
and two outer circles. In 72 evaluations (including explicit nonzero
witnesses) the largest scaled error in the boundary was
(1.7\times10\^{}\{-14\}), the largest physical-residue mismatch
(6.2\times10\^{}\{-14\}), and the largest unphysical residue
(7.5\times10\^{}\{-15\}).

For the full Yukawa calculation, all 20 pinned fixtures and both null
directions independently gave six distinct moving poles, with nonzero
(B\_\infty) in all 40 cases. Extracting the residues by small circles
and restoring the analytic boundary gave a maximum scaled reconstruction
error of (1.1\times10\^{}\{-13\}). On four fixtures, both shifts and
three (\xi) values, the separate Fourier-action graph contraction
confirmed the outer-contour boundary within (7.5\times10\^{}\{-11\}).
This checks the signs of the four calculated pieces without using gauge
identities to simplify the test graph sum. Errors are numerical
residuals, not theoretical uncertainties.

This result does not cover arbitrary broken gauge groups, non-Abelian
charged channels, all choices of shifted legs, ALT, loop amplitudes,
widths or complex physical poles. It does not define a gauge-independent
helicity fraction for a generic off-shell current. The natural next test
for a more general article is to determine how the witness and hard-line
cancellation change when the neutral commuting bridge is replaced by a
charged bridge with genuine gauge self-interactions. Before any
submission, compare against the closest related versions in the
literature and have a human author reproduce the amplitude identities.
